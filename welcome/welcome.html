C ClipRoots

Turn highlights into useful notes.

Here's how to go from "that's worth keeping" to a published clip with your own take on it, in a few seconds.

  1. 1

    Select text, then clip it

    Highlight a passage anywhere on the web and click the Clip this button that appears. Prefer the keyboard or your right-click menu? Both work too:

    Ctrl+Shift+Y (⌘+Shift+Y on Mac) or Right-click → "Clip this with ClipRoots"
  2. 2

    Clip video and audio too

    On a video or audio player, click 🎬 Clip video to start timing, then click ⏹ Stop when you're done. The same right-click menu works on videos, and you can hide the button mid-recording if it's in the way - an "Unhide" pill stays at the bottom of the screen the whole time.

  3. 3

    Add your take and publish

    Every clip you make joins one pending list. Open ClipRoots from the toolbar icon (or the side panel opens on its own after a clip), write a comment, and publish - alone or as a list.

  4. 4

    Sign in to keep it all

    Signing in saves your clips across devices, lets you follow people, keep clips private, and share a private clip with mutual followers.

  5. 5

    Organize, export, or back it up

    Tag your clips and sort them into folders to find them later, grab a ready-made APA, MLA, or Chicago citation with one click, or export a clip as a text file, a PDF (saved straight to your Downloads folder), or to Notion - right from the Me tab.

  6. 6

    Turn a downloaded PDF into a Google Doc

    ClipRoots doesn't export straight to Google Docs, but Google Drive will convert a PDF for you in a few clicks:

    1. Go to drive.google.com and upload the PDF you downloaded (drag it in, or use "New → File upload").
    2. Right-click the uploaded file and choose "Open with" → "Google Docs".
    3. Drive creates an editable Doc from it automatically - no typing required.

You can always re-open this guide from the Compose tab's "How it works" button.
