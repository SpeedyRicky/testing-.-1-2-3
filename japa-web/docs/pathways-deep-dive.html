Tourist Gamble

The visitor route is the one Nigerians reach for when study and Express Entry look out of range. In the game you land at Toronto Pearson with up to 180 days, savings and a cousin's couch, and you need three employer leads before the money or the clock runs out. Working is an instant fail.

Kawosoke: the asylum sub-path

"Kawosoke" (raise your hands) is the slang for claiming asylum. It is the advice tourists hear most from mentors like Wey Sabi. The game treats it as a real legal process: a true story with evidence can win; a bought story always fails.

The flow as built

  1. Where you claim: at the Pearson counter on arrival (CBSA secondary inspection) or later from the dashboard through the IRCC portal.
  2. What you fear: "nothing, I came for work" (honest exit, back to the dashboard); a bought story from Wey Sabi's paralegal (CAD 1,500); a real sexual-orientation story; real gender-based violence; real conflict in the North (open only if the player's state is Borno, Yobe, Adamawa, Zamfara, Katsina, Kaduna, Sokoto, Niger, Benue or Plateau); or village people.
  3. Who helps: Legal Aid or a refugee clinic (+evidence, +credibility), the paralegal (−CAD 800, −credibility), or a shelter bed.
  4. Month 6: work legally, gather evidence, post the story on TikTok (−credibility), or fly home (claim over).
  5. Hearing prep: fix the form line by line, memorise it like a WAEC answer, or wing it.
  6. Room 7: answer straight, embellish (−25 credibility) or cry on Wey Sabi's cue.
  7. Decision: bought story → misrepresentation; village people → no nexus; credibility under 50 → refused; then the IFA question; enough evidence → protected person, otherwise internal flight alternative.

Claim-only random events (moved to a hotel in Niagara Falls, Wey Sabi's "consultation fee", the family group retelling your story, the first snowstorm) can strike only after a claim is filed.

What makes players run it again

A clock on everything180 tourist days, age points that drop every birthday, the 60-day ITA window, the one-year asylum bar. Every route already has one; show it on screen.
Near misses"469: one point short of the healthcare cutoff." Showing the gap after a draw makes the next run feel winnable. Next step: print the gap in the draw-night result.
Variable rewardsLead rolls on the dashboard, draw nights, random events gated by state. Keep good surprises in the mix (a scholarship email, a Wey Sabi tip that works).
Temptation with a priceEvery route offers a shortcut (leaked IELTS, parked money, bought story). They always fail, loudly, with the real penalty. Players try them once for the ending.
Collect the endingsEach ending is a rank with its own glyph and a Post-on-X line. Next step: a rank cabinet that shows which endings you have found.
It's about youYour name, avatar, state, transport, relative in Canada and job category all change the run. A Borno player sees choices a Lagos player can't.
Routes feed each otherStudent carries into Express Entry; Tourist carries into the LMIA boardroom. Next: Kawosoke protected person → PR landing; a refused study permit → Tourist Gamble.
Characters people quoteWey Sabi, the paralegal, Mr. Kovacs, Ada-era relatives. Give each one a recurring line so players share them.

Content guardrails for Kawosoke

  • Real persecution is never the joke. LGBTQ Nigerians face prison, women flee real violence, and people in the North lose family to insurgents and bandits. Those choices are written straight and can win.
  • The humour points at the people who sell shortcuts: Wey Sabi, the unlicensed paralegal, the family group chat.
  • Fabrication always fails, with the real penalty named, and the ending says what fake stories cost genuine claimants.
  • The honest "I came for work" answer is always available and costs nothing but time.
  • No step-by-step advice on making a claim look credible. The game shows what the board weighs (consistency, evidence, IFA) and stops there.
  • Keep the "not legal or immigration advice" line on every screen.
